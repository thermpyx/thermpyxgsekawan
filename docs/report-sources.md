# THERMPYX supporting references

Checked on 18 October 2026. References newly checked for this revision are supporting/comparison sources. Repository inspection does not establish that the original authors historically consulted them.

The selection includes **10 open-access journal articles published in 2022–2026** and **two openly accessible 2022 textbooks**. Publisher metadata, licenses and real PDF responses were checked. Article PDFs were read for their abstracts and relevant conceptual passages; these checks are not a systematic review of all their methods. Download links remain with the official publishers.

## Mapping supporting references to the seven topics

The books supply the standard foundations and assumptions. The journal studies supply recent conceptual, diagnostic or research comparisons. This is a revision-source matrix, not a reconstruction of the authors’ undocumented original reading history.

| Theory topic and repository page | Foundation in the books | Recent theory article(s) | What the references support and what must remain qualified |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heat and temperature (`theory/heat.html`) | B1: chapters 13–14; B2: §4.2 | J1 | Separate heat transfer from stored internal energy; discuss heat capacity with its assumptions. Q = mcΔT assumes constant specific heat and no phase change; visual particles are illustrative. |
| Zeroth law (`theory/zeroth-law.html`) | B2: §2.2.2; B1: §13.1 | J1 (heat-transfer misconceptions context only) | Thermal equilibrium is transitive and underlies temperature comparison; the direct law source is B2. A mixed-sample equilibrium temperature additionally requires an energy balance and heat-capacity assumptions; it is not the zeroth-law equation. |
| Work and energy (`theory/work-energy.html`) | B2: §§4.1, 4.3; B1: §§15.1–15.2 | J2 | Internal energy is a state property while heat/work depend on the process; pressure-volume work needs an identified path. W = PΔV assumes constant relevant boundary pressure; expansion is positive under the project convention. |
| First law (`theory/first-law.html`) | B2: §4.4; B1: §15.1 | J2; J4 | For a closed system with negligible macroscopic kinetic/potential changes, ΔU = Q − W using heat-in/work-by-system signs. These references support the concept and diagnosis; they do not show the website improved learning. |
| Thermodynamic processes (`theory/processes.html`) | B2: §§3.1, 4.3–4.4, 6.7.4; B1: §15.2 | J2; J4 | Distinguish isothermal, isobaric, isochoric and reversible adiabatic conditions and path-dependent work. PV^γ = constant requires an ideal gas, reversible adiabatic process and effectively constant heat-capacity ratio. |
| Second law and entropy (`theory/second-law.html`) | B2: §§6.3–6.5; B1: §§15.3–15.6 | J3 | Distinguish system entropy change from entropy generation and state the reversible-isothermal conditions for ΔS = Qrev/T. Carnot efficiency uses absolute temperatures and is an upper bound for the specified reservoir pair; the program’s 80% factor is illustrative. |
| Third law (`theory/third-law.html`) | B2: §2.2.2 (absolute-temperature background only); B1: §15.4 (absolute-zero/Carnot background only) | J5 | Use §III.B to distinguish the Nernst/Planck entropy formulation from unattainability and use current cooling research to discuss limitations on reaching absolute zero. J5 does not establish the site’s S ∝ T³ illustration as universal. The report must distinguish the perfect-crystal entropy statement from the unattainability principle and avoid claiming these introductory book sections derive the full third law. |

## Correction notices and verification

J2 has [a 2024 erratum](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.20.029902); J4 has [a 2024 erratum](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.20.029901). Both one-page correction PDFs were read. They clarify that some introductory students answered the first 48 or last 52 items in split administrations rather than the complete 78-item instrument; J4 also provides the separated group statistics. Do not claim all participants completed every item. The correction notices are supplementary and are not counted among the ten research articles.

All ten selected publisher article pages and their PDF endpoints returned HTTP 200; all downloaded article files began with `%PDF-`. OpenStax’s PDF returned HTTP 200 and was checked against its ISBN/copyright page. The BCcampus download returned HTTP 200 with a PDF signature; only the first 8192 bytes were read for that download check. Full article identifiers, dates, author names, PDF hashes and limitations are in [report-sources.json](report-sources.json).

The alternative Springer paper *Proof of the Nernst theorem* (2025; DOI 10.1140/epjp/s13360-025-06503-w) was **excluded**: Crossref metadata was available, but the publisher redirect through `idp.springer.com` was blocked with proxy CONNECT HTTP 403. No blocked candidate is represented as a verified downloadable source.

No third-party article or book PDFs are added to the web app. Official external download links keep the report and application repository lightweight.
