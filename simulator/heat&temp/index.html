Heat and Temperature

Heat is energy transferred between objects because of a temperature difference. When heat is supplied to a material, its temperature can increase.

Q = m × c × ΔT
Q Heat supplied (J)
m Mass (kg)
c Specific heat (J/kg·K)
ΔT Temperature change (K)
What happens when heat is supplied?

When a material receives heat, the energy of its particles increases. The particles move more actively, which can result in an increase in temperature.

What is specific heat?

Specific heat is the amount of heat required to increase the temperature of 1 kg of a material by 1 K.

Why does mass affect temperature change?

For the same amount of supplied heat, a larger mass requires more energy to produce the same temperature increase.

How do I read the simulation?

Move the cursor over the water, flame, thermometer, or particles to see their current values. You can also move the cursor over the graph to inspect a specific point.

Example

Suppose 42,000 J of heat is supplied to 1 kg of water. Since the specific heat of water is approximately 4,200 J/kg·K:

ΔT = Q / (m × c)

ΔT = 42,000 / (1 × 4,200)

ΔT = 10 K

If the initial temperature is 25 °C, the final temperature becomes approximately 35 °C.

Key Idea

The amount of temperature change depends on the supplied heat, mass, and specific heat of the material.
