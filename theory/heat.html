THEORY 01

Heat & Temperature

Learn the basic concepts of heat, temperature, specific heat capacity, and temperature change in thermodynamic systems.

01

What is Heat?

Heat is energy transferred from one object or system to another because of a difference in temperature.

Heat always flows naturally from a region with higher temperature to a region with lower temperature until thermal equilibrium is reached.

02

What is Temperature?

Temperature is a physical quantity that indicates the thermal state of a substance.

Temperature is commonly measured using units such as Celsius (°C), Kelvin (K), and Fahrenheit (°F).

03

Specific Heat Capacity

Specific heat capacity is the amount of heat required to increase the temperature of one kilogram of a substance by one kelvin.

Symbol c J/kg·K
04

Temperature Change

The change in temperature is the difference between the final temperature and the initial temperature.

Formula ΔT = Tfinal − Tinitial
05

Heat Equation

The amount of heat transferred when the temperature of a substance changes can be calculated using:

Q = m × c × ΔT
Q Heat energy (J)
m Mass (kg)
c Specific heat capacity (J/kg·K)
ΔT Temperature change (K)
06

Example Problem

A 2 kg object has a specific heat capacity of 4186 J/kg·K. Its temperature increases by 10 K. Determine the amount of heat transferred.

Given:

m = 2 kg

c = 4186 J/kg·K

ΔT = 10 K

Formula:

Q = m × c × ΔT

Q = (2)(4186)(10)

Q = 83,720 J
