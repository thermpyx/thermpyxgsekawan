01

Thermodynamic Processes

A thermodynamic process changes a system from an initial state to a final state, altering properties such as pressure (P), volume (V), and temperature (T).

Four important idealized processes are isothermal, isobaric, isochoric, and adiabatic.

02

Isothermal: Constant Temperature

The temperature remains constant. For an ideal gas, PV is constant and internal-energy change is zero. Consequently, heat entering the system can equal the work done by the gas.

T = constant   ·   P1V1 = P2V2
ΔU = 0   ⇒   Q = W

Example: a piston expands very slowly while staying in thermal contact with a heat reservoir. Heat enters to maintain the temperature.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A gas expands isothermally from 2 L to 4 L at an initial pressure of 200 kPa. Determine its final pressure.

P1V1 = P2V2
P2 = (200 × 2) / 4

P₂ = 100 kPa

03

Isobaric: Constant Pressure

Pressure remains constant while volume and temperature may change. A gas heated in a cylinder with a freely moving piston can expand under nearly constant pressure.

P = constant   ·   V1/T1 = V2/T2
W = P(V2 − V1)
WORKED EXAMPLE

Gas at 100 kPa changes volume from 2 m³ to 5 m³. Determine the work done by the gas.

W = 100,000 × (5 − 2)

W = 300,000 J = 300 kJ

04

Isochoric: Constant Volume

The volume remains constant, so there is no pressure-volume boundary work. Heat transferred changes internal energy.

V = constant   ·   ΔV = 0   ·   W = 0
ΔU = Q

Example: heating gas in a closed, rigid container raises temperature and pressure without changing its volume.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A system absorbs 400 J of heat during an isochoric process. Find the internal-energy change.

ΔU = Q − W = 400 − 0

ΔU = +400 J

05

Adiabatic: No Heat Exchange

No heat transfers between the system and its surroundings during the process. Work therefore changes internal energy.

Q = 0   ⇒   ΔU = −W

If gas expands and performs work, its internal energy can decrease. During rapid compression, work done on the gas can increase its temperature.

Example: when air is compressed quickly in a bicycle pump, there may be too little time for significant heat exchange with the surroundings. Rapid expansion can produce cooling.

07

Reading a P-V Diagram

A pressure-volume diagram helps illustrate thermodynamic processes:

  • Isobaric: horizontal line (constant pressure).
  • Isochoric: vertical line (constant volume).
  • Isothermal: a downward-sloping curve.
  • Adiabatic: generally a steeper downward curve than isothermal.
Work done by the system = area under the P-V curve
