02

Work in Thermodynamics

Work is energy transferred when a force causes displacement of a system boundary. As gas expands in a cylinder, it pushes the piston outward and performs work on the surroundings.

For a process at constant pressure:

W = PΔV = P(V2 − V1)
WWork (J)
PPressure (Pa)
ΔVVolume change (m³)
  • Expansion: ΔV > 0, so work by the gas is positive.
  • Compression: ΔV < 0, so work by the gas is negative.

More generally, work is represented by the area under the pressure-volume curve:

W = ∫ P dV
03

Internal Energy

Internal energy (U) is the total microscopic energy stored in a system, especially the kinetic and potential energy of its particles.

ΔU = U2 − U1

For an ideal gas, internal energy primarily depends on temperature. Internal energy is a state property, unlike heat and work, which describe ways energy crosses the system boundary.

04

Heat, Work & Internal Energy

Heat (Q)Energy transferred because of a temperature difference.
Work (W)Energy transferred through mechanical work.
Internal Energy (U)Energy stored within the system.

Heat and work are not forms of energy stored in the system. Their transfer can change internal energy.

05

Connection to the First Law

With positive Q representing heat entering the system and positive W representing work done by the system:

ΔU = Q − W
  • Q > 0: heat enters the system.
  • Q < 0: heat leaves the system.
  • W > 0: the system performs work.
  • W < 0: work is done on the system.

For example, compressing air using a bicycle pump involves work done on the air, which can make the pump feel warm. Similarly, heating water on a stove transfers energy to the water as heat.

06

Example Problem

WORKED EXAMPLE

A gas receives 500 J of heat and performs 200 J of work on its surroundings. Find its change in internal energy.

Given: Q = 500 J and W = 200 J.

ΔU = Q − W = 500 − 200

ΔU = +300 J; the internal energy increases.

07

Key Takeaway

Work transfers energy mechanically; internal energy is stored in the system. Heat and work affect it through the First Law.
