01

Thermal Equilibrium

The Zeroth Law describes thermal equilibrium between systems and provides the foundation for defining and measuring temperature.

If system A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B is in thermal equilibrium with C, then A is also in thermal equilibrium with C.

A ↔ B    ·    B ↔ C    ⇒    A ↔ C

Thermal equilibrium occurs when two systems have the same temperature and there is no net heat transfer between them. Although formulated after the First and Second Laws, it is called the Zeroth Law because this idea is more fundamental.

02

Key Concepts

Temperature is a quantity that indicates the thermal state of a system. If two objects with different temperatures can exchange heat, energy flows from the hotter object to the colder one until they reach the same temperature.

  • Higher temperature → lower temperature: spontaneous heat transfer.
  • Equal temperatures → thermal equilibrium, with no net heat transfer.
03

Fundamental Relationship

The Zeroth Law has no separate energy-balance equation. Its essential relationship can be written as:

TA = TB   and   TB = TC
∴   TA = TC

Here, T denotes temperature.

04

Everyday Applications

Measuring body temperature with a thermometer. When placed against the body, the thermometer may initially have a different temperature. Heat flows until the body and thermometer reach equilibrium, allowing it to indicate body temperature.

Tbody = Tthermometer
  • Air-conditioner thermostats
  • Refrigerator thermometers
  • Water thermometers
  • Temperature sensors in engines and machines
05

Example Problem

WORKED EXAMPLE

Thermometer A is in thermal equilibrium with water B. When A is used to measure object C, it indicates the same temperature. What can be concluded about water B and object C?

Given the first equilibrium: TA = TB.

The second measurement gives: TA = TC.

TB = TC

Water B and object C have the same temperature.

Key Takeaway
